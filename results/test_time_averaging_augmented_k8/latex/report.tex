       \large \texttt{map} and \texttt{random\_augmented} tie at matched compute on $k\!=\!16$;

\begin{abstract}
We compare three ways of resolving the sign + basis ambiguity of Laplacian
eigenvector positional encodings (LapPE) on the ogbg-molpcba molecular
benchmark: (i) deterministic canonicalization (\texttt{map}, \texttt{oap}),
(ii) training-time random augmentation (\texttt{random\_augmented}), and
(iii) random augmentation \emph{plus} test-time averaging over $K$ random
draws of the eigenvector ambiguity group. Five sweeps (108 runs):
Sweeps~1--3 under a fixed $200$-epoch / patience-$15$ protocol at
$k\!\in\!\{3,8,16\}$; Sweep~4 lifts the budget to $500$~ep / no early
stopping at $k\!=\!16$ for \texttt{random\_augmented} only; \textbf{Sweep~5
gives \texttt{map} and \texttt{oap} the same $500$-ep budget at $k\!=\!16$}
(matched compute).

\textbf{Honest bottom line, at matched compute on $k\!=\!16$ / GIN / molpcba
/ 3 seeds:}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \texttt{map} and \texttt{random\_augmented} are \emph{statistically
      tied} at every hidden dim tested (Welch $p\!\geq\!0.24$ at every
      $h$).
\item Both \emph{do} significantly beat \texttt{oap} at $h\!=\!128$
      ($p\!<\!0.001$); \texttt{random\_augmented} beats \texttt{oap} at
      $h\!=\!512$ ($p\!\approx\!0.04$); at $h\!=\!16$ all three are tied.
\item Earlier readings (e.g.\ ``random\_augmented dominates with
      sufficient compute'' from Sweep~4 alone, or ``\texttt{oap} wins
      everywhere'' from Sweep~3 alone) were apples-to-oranges
      artefacts of unmatched compute --- \texttt{map} also gains
      $+0.0088$ to $+0.0144$~AP under matched compute, indistinguishable
      in scale from \texttt{random\_augmented}'s gain.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Scope: these conclusions apply to $k\!=\!16$ only.} The matched-compute
test was not run at $k\!\in\!\{3,8\}$. The Sweep~1
$k\!=\!3$ / $h\!=\!512$ \texttt{oap}-edge is not re-tested. Spielman, MaxAbs,
Abs, and the bare \texttt{none} baseline are out of scope. ogbg-molpcba
only; GIN backbone only; $h\!\in\!\{16,128,512\}$ only; $3$ seeds.
\end{abstract}

%==============================================================================
\section{What problem are we solving?}
%==============================================================================

Graph neural networks like GIN benefit from positional encodings (PE) that
tell the network ``where'' each node lives in the graph's spectrum. The
standard recipe is to compute the $k$ smallest non-trivial eigenvectors of
the graph Laplacian and concatenate them to each node feature. This works,
but eigenvectors are not unique: for any simple eigenvalue, the eigenvector
is defined only up to a sign $(\pm 1)$; for a multiplicity-$m$ eigenvalue,
\emph{any orthogonal change of basis $Q\in O(m)$} produces an equally valid
eigenbasis. So the same graph, computed on different days or different
machines, can yield numerically different PE.

Three ways to handle this ambiguity:

\paragraph{Canonicalization.} Pick one element of the ambiguity group
deterministically by some rule. Examples:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
  \item \texttt{map} --- maximum-absolute-position rule per column.
  \item \texttt{oap} --- ordered-absolute-position rule, more robust at
        multiplicity blocks.
\end{itemize}
The model sees the \emph{same} PE every epoch and at test time.

\paragraph{Random augmentation.}
(\texttt{random\_augmented}.) Each training-time forward pass samples a
fresh random element of the ambiguity group and applies it to the PE. The
network never sees the same canonical ordering twice and is forced to learn
representations that are invariant to sign flips and $O(m)$ rotations. At
test time the standard practice is to either pick one canonical ordering
(degrades) or do a single un-augmented pass (mismatches training).

\paragraph{Random augmentation $+$ test-time averaging.}
(\texttt{random\_augmented} with $K$-sample averaging at test time.) For
each test graph, sample $K$ independent draws from the ambiguity group,
forward-pass the network on each, and average the predicted probabilities
before computing the metric. This is the test-time analog of training-time
augmentation --- you take an empirical Monte-Carlo estimate of the
\emph{group-invariant} prediction. Inspired by the
\texttt{test\_with\_randomized\_invariance} helper in
\href{https://github.com/jwsiegel2510/Sn-invariant-weighted-frames}%
{Siegel \emph{et al.}, weighted-frames}; we adapt it from $S_n$ to the
LapPE ambiguity group $\prod_i \mathrm{O}(m_i)$.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The augmentation rng is seeded as
$(\text{base\_seed} \cdot 1{,}000{,}003 + s) \times 2{,}654{,}435{,}761
\oplus \text{graph\_idx}$, so the aug-averaged metric is bit-reproducible
for a given $(\text{seed}, K)$. The training loop seeding (\texttt{torch.manual\_seed},
cuDNN determinism, \texttt{worker\_init\_fn}) is what
\texttt{src/training/seed\_everything} provides --- not bit-reproducible
across hardware/CUDA versions; see \texttt{scripts/train\_molecular.py}
for the exact call. Per-seed results may therefore differ slightly
from a clean re-run on different hardware.

\paragraph{Asymmetric test-time protocol.}
For \texttt{map}/\texttt{oap}, the test loader returns the deterministic
canonical ordering. For \texttt{random\_augmented}, the test loader
returns the eigenvectors with the \emph{cached} sign pattern from the
one-time \texttt{lappe\_cache} build (essentially one arbitrary-but-fixed
sign assignment per graph), \emph{not} a fresh draw. This makes the
single-pass \texttt{best\_test\_ap} comparable across methods in the
sense of being deterministic, but the \texttt{random\_augmented} pattern
is not its training-distribution mode --- which is why we also report
\texttt{best\_test\_ap\_aug16} (averaged over $K\!=\!16$ random draws
from the ambiguity group at test time).

\subsection{Sweep~1: $k=3$, $K=8$ --- random augmentation wins; one apparent \texttt{oap} edge (likely compute artefact)}

ogbg-molpcba molecules, so the ambiguity group is just
$\mathbb{Z}_2^3 = 8$. We picked $K\!=\!8$ to cover it exactly in
expectation. \texttt{random\_augmented} wins at $h\!=\!16$ and $h\!=\!128$;
\texttt{oap} narrowly takes $h\!=\!512$. This single $k\!=\!3$/$h\!=\!512$
\texttt{oap} edge is the only cell across the four sweeps where
\texttt{random\_augmented} appears to lose under fixed compute --- and
Sweep~4 (Table~\ref{tab:k16long}) shows that the analogous Sweep~3
\texttt{oap} wins at $k\!=\!16$ disappear once \texttt{random\_augmented}

\subsection{Sweep~3: $k=16$, $K=16$ --- under fixed compute, \texttt{oap} appears to win at every hidden dim}

At $k\!=\!16$ the sign group is $\mathbb{Z}_2^{16}\!=\!65{,}536$. The
test-time sample budget is the same $K\!=\!16$ as Sweep~2 (now sampling
$\sim$0.024\% of the group). Under the same $200$-epoch / patience-$15$
protocol as Sweeps~1--2, the result reverses: \texttt{oap} now wins at
every hidden dim by margins of $+0.0048$ to $+0.0094$ over

show, \emph{this $k\!=\!16$ \texttt{oap}-wins reading is not robust}: it
disappears once \texttt{random\_augmented} is trained long enough to
internalise invariance over the much larger ambiguity group.

\subsection{Sweep~4: $k=16$, $500$ epochs, no early stopping --- random augmentation wins everywhere}

The Sweep~3 result --- \texttt{oap} winning at every $h$ at $k\!=\!16$
--- raised an obvious question: was \texttt{random\_augmented} losing
because canonicalization is fundamentally better at large $k$, or
because $200$ epochs / patience $15$ wasn't enough budget for the
network to internalise invariance over $\mathbb{Z}_2^{16}\!=\!65{,}536$
sign patterns? This sweep tests the latter: same $k\!=\!16$, same $h$
grid, but $500$ epochs and disabled early stopping
($\text{patience}\!=\!999$). Only the \texttt{random\_augmented} arm
(canonicalization baselines from Sweep~3 stand).

%==============================================================================
\section{Interpretation}
%==============================================================================

\subsection{Matched compute on $k\!=\!16$: \texttt{map} $\approx$ \texttt{random\_augmented} $>$ \texttt{oap}}

The five sweeps together tell a more nuanced story than any subset.
Under fixed $200$-epoch compute (Sweeps~1--3), \texttt{random\_augmented}
wins at $k\!\in\!\{3, 8\}$ but loses at $k\!=\!16$. Sweep~4 alone
(only re-running \texttt{random\_augmented} at $500$ ep) suggested this
was a compute artefact. Sweep~5 then demonstrates that \texttt{map}
also gains $+0.0088$ to $+0.0144$~AP under matched compute --- as much
or more than \texttt{random\_augmented} did. The honest matched-compute
verdict at $k\!=\!16$ (Welch's $t$-test, $n\!=\!3$ per arm):

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{\texttt{map} vs.\ \texttt{random\_augmented} are statistically
      tied} at every $h$ tested:
      $h\!=\!16$ $\Delta\!=\!+0.0025$ ($p\!\approx\!0.52$);
      $h\!=\!128$ $\Delta\!=\!+0.0007$ ($p\!\approx\!0.24$);
      $h\!=\!512$ $\Delta\!=\!-0.0015$ ($p\!\approx\!0.44$).
      Nominal winners flip across $h$ but no comparison is significant.
\item \texttt{oap} loses significantly to \texttt{random\_augmented} (and to
      \texttt{map}) at \textbf{$h\!=\!128$} ($\Delta\!\sim\!-0.008$~AP,
      $p\!<\!0.001$), and to \texttt{random\_augmented} at $h\!=\!512$
      ($p\!\approx\!0.04$). At $h\!=\!16$ all three are tied.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The recommendation is therefore narrow:} on $k\!=\!16$ /
ogbg-molpcba / GIN with $\geq\!500$~ep training, choose between
\texttt{map} and \texttt{random\_augmented} on grounds other than test
AP (they're indistinguishable at $3$ seeds); avoid \texttt{oap} when
$h\!\geq\!128$. Without matched compute, head-to-head verdicts are
\emph{not reliable}: any of the three methods can look ``best'' if one
arm is given more epochs than the others.

\subsection{Why does training compute matter so much at large $k$?}

Sweep~5 demonstrates that \emph{both} \texttt{random\_augmented} and
\texttt{map} need substantially more than $200$ epochs to converge at
$k\!=\!16$, while \texttt{oap} converges much earlier. Two complementary
mechanisms:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item For \texttt{random\_augmented}: the ambiguity group
      $\mathbb{Z}_2^k$ grows exponentially in $k$ ($8 \to 256 \to
      65{,}536$). Internalising invariance over $65{,}536$ sign patterns
      simply takes more gradient steps than over $8$.
\item For \texttt{map}: the deterministic ordering rule depends on
      eigenvector values, which become less stable as $k$ grows (more
      eigenvectors, more chances for near-ties in the ordering rule).
      The network needs more epochs to learn around this instability.
      \texttt{oap}'s ordering rule is more numerically robust and
      doesn't have this issue.
\end{itemize}

This explains the Sweep~3 ($200$ ep) result that ``\texttt{oap} wins
everywhere at $k\!=\!16$'': it was the only one of the three methods
that had finished converging at that compute budget. Once the
under-trained methods are given equal compute, two of the three
($\texttt{map}$ and $\texttt{random\_augmented}$) move ahead.

There is also a side-benefit visible in seed std (Table~\ref{tab:k16long}
and Table~\ref{tab:k16matched}): long-trained \texttt{random\_augmented}
is the most seed-stable arm of the three, and \texttt{map} at $h\!=\!128$
collapses to $\pm 0.0005$ std --- both consequences of full convergence,
not method-specific properties.

\subsection{Test-time averaging matters less than longer training}

The aug-averaged $\Delta$ across the four sweeps:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{l r r r}
\toprule
sweep & $k$ & $K$ & best $\Delta$ aug at $h\!=\!512$ \\
\midrule
$1$ ($200$ ep)            &  $3$ & $ 8$ & $+0.0002$ \\
$2$ ($200$ ep)            &  $8$ & $16$ & $+0.0005$ \\
$3$ ($200$ ep)            & $16$ & $16$ & $+0.0006$ \\
\textbf{$4$ ($500$ ep, no ES)} & $16$ & $16$ & $\mathbf{-0.0002}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Under fixed compute (Sweeps~1--3) the $\Delta$ grows with the size of
the ambiguity group --- bigger group, more variance for averaging to
suppress. Under sufficient compute (Sweep~4) the $\Delta$ collapses to
zero (and is even slightly negative within seed std). \emph{Test-time
averaging is a band-aid for under-trained invariance: once the network
has fully internalised invariance, averaging adds nothing.} Spending
the budget on training is strictly more valuable than on test-time
sampling.

\subsection{Why the prior \texttt{oap} ``wins'' were not robust}

At matched compute (Sweep~5), \texttt{oap} is the worst of the three
methods at every $h$ at $k\!=\!16$. Its $200$-epoch ``wins'' (Sweep~3)
were not because \texttt{oap} produced better representations --- they
were because \texttt{oap} happened to converge faster than the
alternatives at the rate-limiting hyperparameter ($200$ ep / patience
$15$). The two cells where \texttt{oap} appeared to win in the $200$-epoch
sweeps were:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item $k\!=\!3$, $h\!=\!512$: \texttt{oap} $0.2431$ vs \texttt{random\_aug}
      $0.2395$. The prior PDF flagged that \texttt{oap}'s seed std there
      ($\pm 0.0022$) was unusually tight and called the win narrow.
\item $k\!=\!16$, all $h$: discussed above --- a compute artefact.
\end{itemize}

Sweep~4 confirms the second case is not robust; we did not directly
re-test the first ($k\!=\!3$, $h\!=\!512$) under longer training, but
the same mechanism (insufficient invariance budget) is the simplest
hypothesis. Either way, with the saturation test in hand the
\emph{best test AP at every $(k, h)$ in the entire study} comes from
\texttt{random\_augmented}.

%==============================================================================
\section{Main conclusions}
%==============================================================================

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{At matched compute on $k\!=\!16$, \texttt{map} and
      \texttt{random\_augmented} are statistically tied at every $h$.}
      Welch's $t$-test on the matched-compute Sweep~5 vs.\ Sweep~4
      data ($n\!=\!3$ per arm): $p\!\approx\!0.52$ at $h\!=\!16$,
      $p\!\approx\!0.24$ at $h\!=\!128$, $p\!\approx\!0.44$ at $h\!=\!512$.
      Nominal winners flip across $h$ but no \texttt{map} vs.\ \texttt{random\_augmented}
      comparison reaches $p\!<\!0.05$.

\item \textbf{Both \texttt{map} and \texttt{random\_augmented} significantly
      beat \texttt{oap} at $h\!=\!128$.} ($p\!<\!0.001$,
      $\Delta\!\sim\!-0.008$~AP). \texttt{random\_augmented} also
      beats \texttt{oap} at $h\!=\!512$ ($p\!\approx\!0.04$). At
      $h\!=\!16$ all three are tied. So at $k\!=\!16$ / large $h$,
      \texttt{oap} is the clear loser; the choice between the other two
      is a tie at this seed count.

\item \textbf{The earlier ``\texttt{random\_augmented} wins everywhere''
      reading from Sweep~4 alone was an apples-to-oranges artefact.}
      Sweep~4 compared $500$-ep \texttt{random\_augmented} to $200$-ep
      \texttt{oap}/\texttt{map}. At matched compute, \texttt{map}
      gains $+0.0088$ to $+0.0144$~AP --- statistically indistinguishable
      from \texttt{random\_augmented}'s gain. The Sweep~3 reading that
      ``\texttt{oap} wins everywhere'' was the same kind of artefact in
      reverse: \texttt{oap} happened to converge faster than the other
      two arms at $200$~ep.

\item \textbf{Test-time ambiguity-group averaging is small but non-zero
      at the protocols tested.} Aug-$\Delta$ is $+0.0001$ to $+0.0008$~AP
      across the four sweeps that report it. We do \emph{not} claim it
      ``collapses to zero on saturation'' --- that would require a
      saturation criterion we cannot verify (see Caveats).

\item \textbf{Practical recipe} for GIN $+$ LapPE on \emph{ogbg-molpcba
      at $k\!=\!16$}: train for $\geq\!500$~ep with disabled early
      stopping. Choose between \texttt{map} and \texttt{random\_augmented}
      by criteria \emph{other than test AP} (they tie at this seed
      count). Avoid \texttt{oap} when $h\!\geq\!128$. \emph{Do not project
      this recipe to other $k$ or other datasets without re-running.}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Caveats (read carefully --- the conclusions above are
      narrower than the headline of any single sweep would suggest).}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{$3$ seeds per cell.} With $n\!=\!3$, even the largest
      matched-compute \texttt{map}-vs-\texttt{random\_augmented}
      difference ($+0.0025$ AP at $h\!=\!16$) is $p\!\approx\!0.52$.
      $5\!+\!$ seeds would be needed to detect $\Delta\!\sim\!0.001$~AP
      with reasonable power.
\item \textbf{Matched compute was tested only at $k\!=\!16$.} The
      $k\!\in\!\{3,8\}$ matched-compute behaviour is unknown. The
      Sweep~1 $k\!=\!3$/$h\!=\!512$ \texttt{oap} edge in particular was
      not re-tested. A planned sibling sweep
      \texttt{results/matched\_compute\_k3\_h512/} was killed at $14$~h
      after $4$ of $9$ runs were finalized externally; the partial
      data is committed to the repo but not summarized here.
\item \textbf{No \texttt{none} baseline.} Bare uncanonicalized eigenvectors
      (\texttt{none}) were not evaluated under any of the five sweeps
      at $k\!\in\!\{8,16\}$. If \texttt{none} is competitive at these
      $k$, the entire framing ``canonicalization vs.\ augmentation''
      is moot. (Sweep $1$ included \texttt{none} at $k\!=\!3$ but only
      at $h\!\leq\!256$; it loses at the smaller $h$.)
\item \textbf{Externally finalized runs.} All $9$ Sweep~4 runs and $6$ of
      $18$ Sweep~5 runs (the wave-2 \texttt{oap} runs --- all three
      seeds at $h\!\in\!\{128, 512\}$) were stopped externally at
      $\sim$T+74~h via \texttt{scripts/finalize\_run.py} from saved
      \texttt{best\_model.pt}. For these $15$ of $27$ $k\!=\!16$-long
      runs, \texttt{best\_epoch} and \texttt{stopped\_epoch} are recorded
      as $-1$ and \texttt{epoch\_log.json} is missing --- val curves
      cannot be plotted, and ``saturation'' cannot be verified beyond
      ``no improvement to \texttt{best\_model.pt} for several hours
      before kill''. The \texttt{best\_test\_ap} numbers themselves are
      exact (eval'd via the same code path as
      \texttt{train\_molecular.py}'s natural finish).
\item \textbf{Asymmetric test-time protocol} for \texttt{random\_augmented}
      (one cached random sign pattern per graph, vs.\
      deterministic-canonical for \texttt{map}/\texttt{oap}). The
      additional \texttt{aug16} averaging metric is reported alongside
      to characterize how much this matters; the $+0.0001$ to $+0.0005$
      range tells us it's small at the configurations tested.
\item \textbf{Restricted scope.} GIN backbone only; ogbg-molpcba only;
      $k\!\in\!\{3,8,16\}$; $h\!\in\!\{16,128,512\}$; \texttt{spielman},
      \texttt{spielman\_partition}, \texttt{maxabs}, \texttt{abs} all
      excluded. Whether matched-compute conclusions extend to other
      datasets, larger $k$, or transformer backbones is not tested.
\item \textbf{500-epoch best-checkpoint selection over $3$ seeds is a
      $\sim$$1500$-checkpoint search per cell.} With \texttt{patience}
      effectively disabled, methods that train more noisily can sample
      more of the val-AP distribution and benefit from the maximum
      statistic. This caveats the matched-compute fairness somewhat;
      a held-out validation split would be needed to address it.
\item \textbf{No multiple-comparisons correction} applied to the
      $p$-values reported above. With $\sim 9$ pairwise comparisons in
      Table~\ref{tab:k16matched}, a Bonferroni-corrected threshold of
      $0.05/9\!\approx\!0.0056$ would still leave the $h\!=\!128$
      \texttt{oap}-loses result significant ($p\!<\!0.001$) but the
      $h\!=\!512$ \texttt{oap}-loses-to-\texttt{rand\_aug} result
      ($p\!\approx\!0.04$) would not.
\item Sweep~4 runs were stopped externally at $\sim\!41$ h and finalized
      from saved \texttt{best\_model.pt} via
      \texttt{scripts/finalize\_run.py} (loads model, runs full
      val+test+aug-avg eval, writes \texttt{results.json} with the same
      schema as \texttt{train\_molecular.py}). The reported
      \texttt{best\_test\_ap} is exact; \texttt{best\_epoch} and
      \texttt{stopped\_epoch} are unknown ($-1$).
\item \texttt{spielman}, \texttt{maxabs}, \texttt{abs}, \texttt{none}
      were out of scope for these four sweeps.
\end{itemize}
